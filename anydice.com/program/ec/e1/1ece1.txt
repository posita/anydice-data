\Sit awhile and listen, and I will tell you a story. A story of undead, of optimizers, of socially-challenged wizards, and of COVID-19.\
\Once upon a time, there lived Dmitriy, a level 9 Arcanist who was researching interesting undead to reanimate for his noble house. A weird disease had triggered a lockdown, forcing Dmitriy into his library. The arcanist has created a perfect plan of raising a perfect undead capable of mass destruction.\
\There was only this tiny, minor, little problem that could potentially destroy his whole plan: Command Undead (spell). With no save, it's possible to snatch any unintelligent undead you see, and even if your orders are in conflict with what it's creator has said, an opposed Charisma check decides who wins.\
\Astonishingly sad has Dmitriy become. Why should he raise anything that can potentially be turned against him and his friends?\
\Then Dmitriy got an idea: all his friends were Arcanists with access to level 2 spells! Their powerful, stunning Charisma of 7 (bonus of -2) will certainly be of great help! Or will it?\
\Well, Dmitriy had 99 friends in his Guild. Every one of them was ready to order the undead creature to follow Dmitriy's orders at all cost. Now, any order conflicting with Dmitriy's orders will trigger exactly 100 opposed Charisma checks. Animating the bone construct has now become safe!\
\Not long after the animation, Bob-the-no-fun-allower, a high-level optimized Sorcerer with a Charisma score of 36 has cast Command Undead on the undead creature, and ordered to attack Dmitriy, which conflicts with Dmitriy's order to stand still and kill everybody on sight whose name isn't Dmitriy.\
\With such a big difference, each of the nerds' chances to win an opposed check is just 3.75%. As you can see below, however, when Bob has to beat each of them he stands no chance. Let's see why.\



CHARISMABONUSBOB: 13
\This is Bob's effective Charisma bonus. Because his Charisma is 36, (36-10)/2=13 is his bonus\
CHARISMABONUSNERD: 3
\Same here for all the nerds with their -2 penalty\
NERDS: 10
\Here we set how many nerds there are\
\That's how many nerds there are\

BOBROLLS: CHARISMABONUSBOB + 1d20
NERDROLLS: CHARISMABONUSNERD + 1d20
\When rolling their checks, both Bob and a nerd roll 1d20 and add their bonus\

OPPOSITION: ((NERDROLLS - BOBROLLS) > -1)

\Then we subtract Bob's check result from the nerd's one. If it's greater than -1, Bob hasn't won this check. For Bob to succeed, he must win every single check against every nerd\
\Now, to understand what happens next, treat this variable as a weird die, or a coin, that can show one of two states: "Bob wins, you lose" (a result of 0) and "Bob doesn't win, you lose" (a result of 1)\
\Again, it's enough for Bob not to win a check just once, then Bob's order is not followed.\
THEYARETOGETHER: NERDS d OPPOSITION
\Now, we roll this weird coin NERDS number of times. The resulting number is the number of nerds that don't lose their checks against Bob.\
NERDSWINNINGCHANCE: THEYARETOGETHER > 0

\As you can see, it's nigh impossible that they lose. That's the power of numbers.\
