function: heroine rolls DIE:n against MOB_ONE:n and MOB_TWO:n {
  result: {(MOB_ONE <= DIE) - (MOB_TWO <= DIE)}
} 
\**
If mob one is hit, then "MOB_ONE <= DIE" evaluates to "1" (meaning "true"); if mob one is not hit, the statement evaluates to "0" (meaning "false"). The same is true for mob two. Thus, the function output is "1" if mob one is hit and mob two is not, "-1" if mob one is not hit but mob two is, or "0" if neither or both mobs are hit.

Calling this function several times and adding the results gives a cumulative picture of the gap in hits taken over the course of several rounds. Negative results mean mob two has suffered more hits, while positive results mean mob one has suffered more hits. Since we don't care which is which in the end (only how far apart the two mobs are at the end of several rounds), in the following output statements I've taken the absolute value of the final total.
**\


output [absolute 6d[heroine rolls 1d30 against 1d30 and 1d30]] named "Gap in hits taken after 6 rounds"
output [absolute 12d[heroine rolls 1d30 against 1d30 and 1d30]] named "Gap in hits taken after 12 rounds"
output [absolute 18d[heroine rolls 1d30 against 1d30 and 1d30]] named "Gap in hits taken after 18 rounds"
output [absolute 24d[heroine rolls 1d30 against 1d30 and 1d30]] named "Gap in hits taken after 24 rounds"

\**
These output statements use d30's instead of d100's due to limits on Anydice.com's processing power. However, this has a negligible impact on the results. All increasing the die size does is reduce the chance of ties, which in turn reduces the chance that the final balance will be 0 by a minuscule amount - for example, the chance for at least 1 hit of difference after 24 rounds is 85.82% with d10's, compared to 85.88% with d30's or 85.89% with d50's.
